# Permanence Rules

**Summary:** **No clean fixes.** Victories shift a balance; they do not restore the world. This is
the rule that keeps a broken world broken for the whole life of the setting, and it has to be written
down before the first plot, because a campaign that "saves the world" ends the world.

## The decision

**What can never be restored here?** Name three to five things — a species, a climate, a road, a
language, a god, a technique — and say what state they are permanently in.

## The test

For any victory a campaign can win, ask: **what is worse, or merely different, afterwards?** If the
answer is "nothing", the win is too clean. Write the trade-off into the plot before the players find
it.

## Worked example

The worldkit's `example-world/world/plot-web/the-ferrymans-debt.md` is a small thread that ends in a
terminal no quest undoes: the debt is settled, the man is sold on, or he is gone. Every ending costs
someone, and none of them restores what was lost before.
